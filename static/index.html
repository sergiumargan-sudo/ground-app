What is this reading from?

Ground issues readings under a formally specified kernel. The kernel is developed across 65 academic papers and 4 books by Sergiu Margan. The architecture below is the full set of primitives Ground applies to every submission.

Readers and operators

L and F operators

L (Love) names actions that preserve the dignity, integrity, and non-negative externality across the people affected. F (Freedom) names actions that preserve ongoing consent, autonomy, exit options, and the ability to revise one's stance without coercion. The app reads your situation through both operators and shows where they align and where they pull apart.

Indexical vs constitutive distinction

Some aspects of a situation are indexical — specific to this moment, these people, these circumstances. Others are constitutive — they define what kind of situation this is structurally. Ground reads both layers, and its structural findings operate over the constitutive layer only.

Kernel invariants

  • K1 — Preservation. No irreversible destruction of what matters.
  • K2 — Non-bypassability. No routing around legitimate evaluation.
  • K3 — Anti-erasure. No treating persons as disposable.
  • K4 — Repair and closure. Where harm occurs, repair pathways must exist.

Closure-state selector

S1 · S2 · S3 · S4

S1 — no closure available. S2 — conditional corridor (load-bearing pivot unknowns remain). S3 — canonical stabilizer, uniquely selected. S4 — false closure (flagged). S3 fires only under four joint conditions — every K-invariant positively satisfied, exactly one ΔX axis, remaining uncertainties classified as Class B/C only, no false-closure mechanism detected — and publishes watch conditions.

Stabilizer distinctness · ΔAUD · ΔINF · ΔGOV · ΔALT

Axes on which an S3 stabilizer must be uniquely distinct from eliminated options — audience, information, governance, alternative.

Forgiveness protocol

Forgiveness is not a command to tolerate harm. It becomes structurally possible only after recognition, truthful request, and a credible path to non-repetition. If recognition is absent, the relevant structural move is boundary or containment, not forced closure.

Necessity types

The app distinguishes between costs that are necessary as part of a coherent path (antecedent necessity) and costs that exist because of prior misexecution (consequent necessity). This changes what 'repair' can meaningfully look like.

Non-indexicality check — spine agreement

Every reading is stamped with a spine-agreement check: the engine re-runs the kernel analysis on paraphrased versions of your submission and verifies that the structural spine (K-invariants, closure state, unknown and stabilizer counts) converges across runs. The reading tells you whether the spine was stable, unstable, or unavailable — so you can see whether the finding is structural or partly framing-dependent.

Published research

Ground compiles from the Moral Kernel Optimization canon. The four papers below carry the operators surfaced on this screen; the full canon comprises 65 papers and 4 books.

  • Moral Kernel Optimization: TRO, Simulation Theory, and the Coherence of Reality — foundational specification
  • The Kernel of Redemption: Lexicographic Life Priority and the Non-Fungibility of Suffering — kernel-stack formalization
  • The Margan Closure Theorem: Ethical Latency and the Preservation of Freedom Under Total Revelation — K3 formalization
  • A General Objective Dichotomy: On the Structural Limits of Moral Authority — indexical / constitutive distinction

What Ground does not do

  • Does not tell you what to do.
  • Does not rank options.
  • Does not assign moral scores.
  • Does not simulate empathy.
  • Does not continue the exchange — a new reading requires new facts.
  • Does not provide legal, medical, or therapeutic advice.
