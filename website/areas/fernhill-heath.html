Roofing in Fernhill Heath, Worcestershire

Fernhill Heath sits on the northern fringe of Worcester, just south of Droitwich along the A38 corridor. It is a semi-rural village with a character that reflects its position between the city and the open Worcestershire countryside — a mix of older period farmhouses and cottages on the original village lanes, alongside inter-war bungalows from the 1930s and post-war residential estates that were built as Worcester expanded northwards. This variety of building ages is one of the things that makes Fernhill Heath interesting from a roofing perspective: the same street can have a Victorian farmhouse requiring slate and lead work and a 1960s bungalow needing a complete fascia and guttering renewal.

The 1930s to 1970s properties in Fernhill Heath are now at a critical age for roofing maintenance. Original felt underlay on roofs from this era has a typical lifespan of around 30–40 years, after which it becomes brittle and prone to tearing, allowing water to pass through to the ceiling even when the tiles above look intact. Ridge tiles on these properties have often had their mortar pointing fail, and the original cast-iron or early UPVC guttering systems have long since reached the end of their useful life. We carry out complete overhauls on this type of property — renewing underlay and battens, repointing ridges, and replacing guttering and fascias in one visit where possible — which is far more cost-effective than addressing each issue separately over several years.
