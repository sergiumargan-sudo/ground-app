Roofing in Chaddesley Corbett, Worcestershire

Chaddesley Corbett is one of Worcestershire’s most complete and visually striking historic villages, designated a conservation area for its exceptional collection of medieval and Tudor buildings. The village street is lined with timber-framed black-and-white buildings that date back centuries, creating a streetscape that makes work here genuinely interesting. The Church of St Cassian has Norman origins and is among the most significant parish churches in the county. The surrounding countryside includes the Chaddesley Wood National Nature Reserve, an ancient oak woodland managed by Natural England, which adds to the special character of the setting. Estate properties on the edge of the village complete the picture of a settlement where architectural quality and historical continuity are genuinely valued.

For a roofer, Chaddesley Corbett presents exactly the same challenges as the other great Worcestershire conservation villages — most notably Ombersley, which shares a similar architectural character. The timber-framed buildings here have natural slate and handmade clay plain tile roofs that cannot simply be replaced with modern alternatives without consent, and in many cases would not be improved by doing so. The original materials have lasted centuries and, when properly maintained, will continue to do so. We source matching handmade plain tiles for repairs, use natural hydraulic lime mortars for all pointing work, and install correctly detailed lead flashings, valleys and soakers. We are comfortable advising on what planning or listed building consent may be required before any work begins, and we always discuss this with owners at the survey stage.
