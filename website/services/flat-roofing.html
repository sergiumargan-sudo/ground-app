What is Dryseal?

Dryseal is a premium GRP (Glass Reinforced Plastic) flat roofing system manufactured in the UK by Hambleside Danelaw. GRP is formed as a single seamless layer directly over the roof deck — no joins, no overlaps, no seams to fail. The result is a completely monolithic waterproof surface that is extremely hard, fire resistant, and capable of handling foot traffic. It looks clean and modern, and is widely regarded as the industry’s top-performing flat roofing material for residential applications.

Unlike older felt systems or cheaper single-ply membranes, Dryseal GRP is a rigid surface that won’t lift, crack, or peel at the edges. Once cured, it bonds permanently to the deck and requires virtually no maintenance over its lifetime.

EPDM Rubber Flat Roofing

EPDM (Ethylene Propylene Diene Monomer) rubber is a highly durable flat roofing membrane offering a lifespan of 20 years or more. It has excellent resistance to UV degradation, temperature fluctuations, and pooling water, and is particularly well-suited to larger flat roof areas such as garage roofs and outbuildings.

It’s installed as a single bonded sheet, eliminating the joints and overlaps that are the weak points of traditional felt systems. EPDM offers outstanding value for money on larger or more utilitarian roof areas.

Felt Flat Roofing

Modern torch-on felt systems use reinforced bitumen membranes (RBM) that offer improved performance over older felt, typically lasting 15–20 years. Felt is often the most economical option for garages, outbuildings and extensions where budget is the primary consideration. Signs Your Flat Roof Needs Attention

Flat roofs should be inspected periodically, particularly after winter. Common signs of failure include:

  • Blistering or bubbling of the roof surface
  • Visible cracking, splitting or crazing of the top surface
  • Ponding water that doesn’t drain within 48 hours
  • Damp patches on the ceiling beneath the flat roof
  • Moss or weed growth through the covering
  • Felt edges lifting away from the fascia or upstand

Many flat roofs can be repaired rather than replaced entirely, particularly if the deck itself is sound. We always assess honestly and will give you both options where applicable.

Repair vs Replacement

Not every flat roof problem requires a full replacement. If the roof deck is in good condition and the issue is localised, we can often carry out a targeted repair using compatible materials. However, if the covering is reaching the end of its lifespan — particularly with older felt systems — or if there are multiple failure points, a full replacement is usually more cost-effective in the long run.

When we carry out a survey, we’ll give you an honest assessment and tell you clearly whether repair or replacement makes more sense, along with indicative costs for each.

Frequently Asked Questions

What warranty do I get on a Dryseal flat roof?

Every Dryseal GRP flat roof installed by SB Margan Roofing comes with a 20-year manufacturer warranty from Hambleside Danelaw. This warranty is independent of the installer — it is registered to the property and issued in the manufacturer’s name. This is separate from and in addition to our workmanship guarantee.

What’s the difference between a manufacturer warranty and a contractor guarantee?

A contractor guarantee is your roofer’s personal promise. If they cease trading, go out of business, or simply become unreachable, the guarantee is worthless. A manufacturer warranty is issued by Hambleside Danelaw directly and remains valid regardless of what happens to the installer. It is tied to the product and the property, not to us.

How long does a Dryseal GRP flat roof installation take?

Most standard extension flat roofs are completed in a single day. We prepare and prime the deck, apply the glass fibre matting, topcoat with Dryseal GRP resin, and the roof is fully weatherproof the same day. Curing completes overnight.

Do I need planning permission for a flat roof?

Re-roofing or replacing an existing flat roof generally falls under permitted development and doesn’t require planning permission, as long as you’re using similar materials. If you’re building a new structure, permitted development rules apply to the structure itself. We can advise on this when we visit.
